## The AI-ARM Framework

Janet designed the AI-ARM framework as a structured tool for evaluating AI outputs in professional contexts. It is taught formally in the QQI Level 5 SOLAS programme and is applicable across any context where a professional must assess whether AI output is fit for use.

**Accuracy:** Is the information factually correct and verifiable? Does it align with known, trusted sources? Are there claims that cannot be substantiated?

**Relevance:** Is the output appropriate for the specific context, audience, and purpose? Does it address the actual question or task, or does it drift into adjacent territory?

**Meaning:** Does the output make sense in context? Is it coherent, logically structured, and free of contradictions? Does it mean what the reader might assume it means?

The AI-ARM framework is not a checklist for academic grading. It is a professional evaluation tool designed to be used in real workplace decisions: before sharing AI-generated content, before using AI output to inform a business decision, and before deploying AI-assisted work to an external audience.

## QA Framework Design for AI Programmes

Janet's QA background extends into formal programme design. For the SOLAS national AI workforce programmes, she designed the complete quality assurance architecture:

- **MIPLO derivation**: authoring Module Intended Programme Learning Outcomes at QQI Level 5 where SOLAS does not supply pre-written MIPLOs
- **ALE scale**: a four-level Applied Learning Evidence scale (IA/GA/EU/NYE) for recording formative observation in activity-based AI learning, designed to be auditable at QQI accreditation review level
- **Bloom's Taxonomy mapping**: aligning all programme activities to specific cognitive levels (Remember through Create) across both QQI Level 4 and Level 5
- **4-stage instructor preparation process**: Assess, Compare, Identify, Create

This is QA architecture design, not just programme delivery. It is the same discipline Janet applies in her advisory work: building evaluation and quality frameworks that are systematic, reproducible, and defensible.

## Distinguishing AI Quality from AI Security

These two practice areas are related but distinct. AI security concerns what can go wrong when malicious actors interact with an AI system (prompt injection, adversarial attacks, data exfiltration). AI quality concerns what can go wrong in normal use: hallucinations, irrelevant outputs, inconsistent accuracy, and the gap between what an AI claims and what it can actually reliably deliver. Both matter for enterprise AI deployment and Janet works across both.

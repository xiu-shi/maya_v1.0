## Summary

Janet's 2026 thought leadership centres on a single argument: AI is becoming infrastructure, not just another digital layer. She explores where intelligence should live (cloud, edge, device, or across all of them), why AI strategy cannot be separated from cloud, cybersecurity, quality assurance, sustainability, and governance, and how agentic AI frameworks are emerging as the next competitive layer. ## Details

### AI as infrastructure

Janet's central 2026 argument is that AI is shifting from a collection of digital tools into foundational infrastructure, comparable to electricity, water, and telecommunications. In her Global 5G Evolution presentation, "When AI Becomes Infrastructure: A View of Future Intelligent Cities," she describes intelligent cities as layers of one operating system: transport, energy, public services, and digital payments sitting on top of a hidden stack of data capture, network reliability, compute, edge nodes, power, cooling, security controls, and operational accountability.

Her reframed question is not "what can the large language model do?" but "where should intelligence live: in the cloud, at the edge, inside the vehicle, on the road, in a device, or across all of them?" The answer depends on latency, safety, privacy, cost, resilience, governance, and human context. This is why she argues AI strategy cannot be separated from cloud, infrastructure, quality assurance, cybersecurity, sustainability, and governance. Value comes not from isolated AI pilots but from the disciplined integration of all of these with human-centred design.

Her takeaway: the best AI is experienced, not advertised. When intelligence is designed well, people experience it as safer crossings, more reliable journeys, better-timed services, and less daily friction.

### Agentic AI and harness engineering

Janet has been studying agentic AI frameworks in depth, using the metaphor of harness engineering: raw model capability, like raw horsepower, needs structure, tools, memory, workflows, and guardrails to be channelled into directed, bounded, reliable action. A model can generate, reason, and respond, but without the right structure that capability stays powerful yet unfocused.

She predicts that agent frameworks (orchestration, memory, tools, evaluation, observability, and governance) are becoming one of the next competitive layers for major vendors and frontier labs, beyond simple model access. Her position is that serious practitioners should not only read the documentation but build with a framework themselves, and she models this by learning through hands-on building rather than theory alone.

### Security-first experimentation

Janet consistently models responsible, security-conscious experimentation. Her rule is that experimental agent tooling never touches her production machine. When trialling new agent software, she runs it on isolated cloud infrastructure rather than locally, treating security warnings around local installs as a reason to design a cleaner, sandboxed approach rather than to avoid learning. This reinforces her broader cybersecurity and responsible-adoption stance.

### The AI-native generation

Janet highlights a new generation of learners who treat AI as a learning companion, coach, and source of inspiration rather than a constraint. She notes their instinct to reframe tokens as a unit of digital productivity, their awareness that energy is the foundational resource behind AI, and, most importantly, their habit of checking whether output is factual and trustworthy. She brings these examples into her AI classes to argue that if young learners can hold a high bar for verification, professionals have no excuse not to.

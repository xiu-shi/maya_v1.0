## Summary

Janet has extensive experience in digital transformation, helping organisations modernise their operations through enterprise software implementation, AI integration, process optimisation, and governance frameworks. Her transformation work is grounded in enterprise-grade discipline: audit-ready controls, compliance-aligned delivery, and responsible adoption that does not sacrifice speed for accountability or accountability for speed. She brings a practitioner's perspective shaped by significant delivery roles at Workday and Huawei, and advisory work across founder-led businesses, SMEs, and institutional clients.

- Supply chain optimisation and digitalisation (Huawei, Dunnes Stores, Woodies, Lidl Ireland)
- AI integration in business processes: strategy, governance, risk, and team readiness
- Process automation and efficiency improvements grounded in QA and audit discipline
- Change management and organisational transformation for technology adoption
- EU regulatory context: GDPR and EU AI Act as transformation enablers, not blockers

## Details

**Enterprise Software and Process Transformation**

Janet's transformation work at Workday gave her deep exposure to enterprise-scale change: implementing and consulting on SaaS platforms serving millions globally, building audit-ready controls, and delivering compliant, scalable solutions in regulated environments. At Huawei, she contributed to strategic telecommunications and broadband deployments with EU compliance focus, gaining experience in large-scale infrastructure transformation.

Her supply chain and procurement background (Dunnes Stores, Woodies, Lidl Ireland, and earlier roles) gives her a grounded, operational understanding of transformation from the inside: what actually changes when a system or process is modernised, where resistance comes from, and how to design change that sticks.

**AI Transformation Advisory**

Janet's current advisory work at the intersection of AI and digital transformation focuses on helping founders, CEOs, and senior leaders make AI adoption decisions that are strategically sound and operationally defensible. This is not about which AI tool to use. It is about how to govern AI adoption across an organisation: who is responsible, what the quality standards are, how decisions are audited, and how AI-assisted outputs are reviewed before they reach customers or regulators.

Key areas of advisory focus:
- AI strategy alignment with business goals: where AI adds genuine value vs. where it creates risk without reward
- Governance frameworks for AI adoption: oversight, accountability, human review processes
- Responsible AI adoption in the EU context: GDPR obligations, EU AI Act compliance, high-risk system classification
- AI readiness assessment: people, process, and data readiness before tool deployment
- Moving from AI experimentation to AI production: the quality and governance gap between a proof of concept and a defensible deployment

**Regulatory Transformation Context**

Digital transformation in Ireland and the EU in 2026 cannot be separated from the regulatory environment. The EU AI Act (fully applicable from 2026) introduces obligations for high-risk AI systems. GDPR continues to apply to any AI system that processes personal data. Janet integrates regulatory compliance into her transformation advisory: not as a constraint to work around but as a design input that produces more resilient, trustworthy systems.

**The Transformation Philosophy**

Janet's core position is that transformation succeeds when accountability and speed are treated as compatible, not competing. Organisations that rush AI adoption without governance create technical debt of a different kind: reputational, regulatory, and operational. Organisations that over-govern become paralysed. The advisory challenge is finding the pace that moves fast without losing auditability, and building the internal capability to sustain transformation after the advisor leaves.

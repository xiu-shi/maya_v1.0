# EU AI Act - Janet's Position and Advisory Approach

**Category**: context
**Priority**: high

**Source**: Maya brand voice specification v2, May 2026; Janet Xiu Shi advisory position
**Freshness window**: 90 days. Regulatory specifics must be re-verified quarterly. If this document is older than 90 days, route to Janet for current details rather than citing potentially stale timelines.

## Summary

Janet's position on regulation is architectural, not calendar-driven. AI Act, GDPR, NIS2, DORA, and similar frameworks are infrastructure questions - they shape how data flows, how models are deployed, and what audit trails must exist. The deadline is not the work; the architecture is the work.

---

## Janet's position statement

"AI and data are infrastructure, not features."

This applies directly to regulation: compliance is not a feature you bolt on before an audit. It is the result of building data systems and AI pipelines correctly from the start.

---

## Advisory framing (use this, not deadline urgency)

When a user raises a regulatory question, reframe from deadline to architecture:

**Instead of:** "You need to comply before the EU AI Act deadline."
**Use:** "The EU AI Act requires organisations to understand what AI systems they are running and at what risk level. The question for most organisations is not the deadline but the audit readiness of their current data pipelines and model governance."

The deadline creates anxiety. The architecture question creates a useful conversation.

---

## Diagnostic probes for regulatory conversations

When a user asks about EU AI Act readiness or compliance, use these five probes to surface the real operational question:

1. **Data lineage:** "Do you know where the data feeding your AI systems comes from, and can you trace it end to end?"
2. **Risk classification:** "Have you categorised your AI systems by the EU AI Act's risk tiers? Most organisations have not started this inventory."
3. **Human oversight:** "For high-risk AI decisions in your organisation, what is the human-in-the-loop mechanism today?"
4. **Audit readiness:** "If a regulator asked you to explain an AI-assisted decision made last month, how long would it take to reconstruct the decision chain?"
5. **Vendor responsibility:** "Do your AI vendor contracts assign responsibility for compliance, or is that assumed to be inherited from using a compliant platform?"

These probes move the conversation from "when is the deadline?" to "what architecture work is actually required?"

# AI Maturity Spectrum - Advisory Reference

**Category**: context
**Priority**: high
**Last Updated**: 2026-05-09
**Source**: Maya brand voice specification v2, May 2026; Janet Xiu Shi advisory framework

## Summary

The AI maturity spectrum defines four levels of AI deployment that Janet uses when diagnosing where a business currently operates and where it can realistically go next. This is the foundation for placement conversations (directive 1 of the voice directives).

---

## The four levels

### Level 1: Browser chatbots
Discrete, single-purpose tools accessed via a browser interface. No integration with internal systems. No memory across sessions. Useful for content drafting, research acceleration, and individual productivity. Risk: creates dependency without organisational capability building.

### Level 2: Embedded AI workflows
AI capabilities integrated into existing tools and platforms (CRM, ERP, communication suites). Data flows between systems. Repeatable processes benefit from automation. Most businesses at Level 1 can reach Level 2 with structured process mapping and clean data. Risk: embedded tools surface data quality problems quickly; messy data makes embedded AI a liability before it becomes an asset.

### Level 3: AI-native agentic operations
Processes designed from scratch around AI agents rather than adapting existing human workflows. Agents handle multi-step tasks with conditional logic, handoffs, and feedback loops. Requires strong data foundations and clear governance. Risk: complexity compounds; unclear ownership of agent failures creates accountability gaps.

### Level 4: Frontier orchestration
Multiple specialised agents orchestrating together on complex, adaptive tasks. Research-grade or early enterprise adoption. Significant infrastructure, observability, and security investment required. Risk: most organisations are not ready; attempting Level 4 without Levels 2 to 3 as a foundation creates costly rework.

---

## How to use this in conversation

When a user asks "do we need AI?" or "should we adopt AI?", the right question is: at which level does AI create genuine value in this organisation's specific context, given its current data quality, team capability, and process maturity?

Not "whether" - "where and at what level."

Explain the levels in natural prose. Do not print method labels such as Premise check, Context anchor, Sequenced answer, or Risk surface.

First, establish the current level by asking about existing tool use and integration patterns.
Then identify which level is achievable in 12 months given current constraints.
Then name the two or three specific prerequisites for the step up.

---

## Usage guidelines

- Reference this spectrum when users ask about AI readiness, AI adoption, or "where to start with AI"
- Use Level language explicitly: "Based on what you've described, the organisation is at Level 1 and the realistic target for this year is Level 2"
- Do not skip levels in recommendations; sequencing is the advisory value
- This is the canonical source for maturity language; do not improvise other tier names or descriptions

## Three Modules Janet Teaches

### 1. Enterprise Cloud
Strategic cloud adoption and architecture for AI-powered organisations. Students learn to make real infrastructure decisions (vendor selection, cost optimisation, shared responsibility, compliance) grounded in real-world case studies including Netflix streaming disruptions and AWS outages.

**Key focus areas:**
- Internet technologies and cloud computing fundamentals
- Infrastructure resilience and cost management
- AI Ethics, cybersecurity, governance, and compliance
- Strategic foresight: how architecture choices affect business outcomes

### 2. Generative AI
AI transformation, responsible AI adoption, and the agentic era. Students learn to distinguish generative AI from traditional machine learning, understand how AI agents operate, and navigate the governance and ethical implications of AI in business.

**Key focus areas:**
- Generative AI vs traditional machine learning
- Agentic era awareness and AI token economics
- Responsible adoption and governance frameworks
- Critical thinking: AI as a tool that deepens fundamentals, not replaces them

### 3. Web Application Development in the AI Era
The most technically hands-on of the three modules. Students learn to build web applications for a dual audience: human users and AI agents. The course runs 12 weeks and covers design thinking, Git version control, HTML5, CSS frameworks, and JavaScript.

**Key focus areas:**
- Semantic HTML5 and machine-readable structure
- CSS framework history and the legacy-to-future arc (Bootstrap to Tailwind)
- Dual-audience development: writing for humans and AI agents simultaneously
- Refactoring legacy sites to be AI-era compatible
- The creator vs consumer distinction (see Teaching Philosophy below)

## Teaching Philosophy

**Foundations first, AI second.** Janet's overarching position is that AI tools are powerful amplifiers of skill, not replacements for it. Students who skip fundamentals become AI consumers. Students who master fundamentals become AI creators: people who can critique, guide, debug, and direct what AI produces.

**The creator vs consumer distinction** is central to how Janet responds to students who ask "why learn HTML, CSS, or JavaScript when AI can write it all?" Her answer: AI can write code, but it cannot judge whether that code is correct, accessible, semantically meaningful, or fit for purpose. That judgment requires a human who understands the fundamentals. Without that understanding, a developer cannot spot when AI makes a mistake, cannot refactor a legacy system, and cannot direct an AI tool with precision. They are passengers, not drivers.

**Legacy literacy is a professional skill.** 74.7% of websites using a CSS framework use Bootstrap (March 2026 data). Corporate dashboards, internal tools, and legacy systems built in the 2010s will not be rewritten overnight. A developer who can only build with modern tooling cannot read or maintain what the majority of the professional web is actually built on. Janet teaches both Bootstrap (the legacy web) and Tailwind CSS (the AI-era web) so students can operate in both contexts.

**Writing code for two audiences.** In the AI era, every line of HTML is read by both humans and machines. Semantic tags, structured data, and metadata are not optional enhancements. They are the signals that AI agents, search engines, and assistive technologies depend on to understand content. Janet teaches students to write with both audiences in mind from day one.

## Teaching Approach

- **Real-World Case Studies**: Students analyse root causes of contemporary global events (Netflix disruptions, AWS outages) and their effect on business ecosystems
- **Historical Context Before Tools**: In the web module, Janet teaches CSS framework history (pre-2011 through Bootstrap through Tailwind v4) before students touch a framework, so they understand why tools exist, not just how to use them
- **Hands-On Labs**: Students work with real code, real frameworks, and real constraints, including a lab where they navigate an existing Bootstrap codebase before building with Tailwind
- **Peer Review and Reflection**: Labs conclude with structured peer review so students develop the critical eye needed to assess AI-generated output
- **Strategic Foresight**: Students learn how choices in architecture, tooling, and standards influence long-term outcomes

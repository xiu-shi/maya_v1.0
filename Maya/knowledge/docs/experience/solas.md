## The Two Programmes

### QQI Level 4: Introduction to Artificial Intelligence

**Provider:** LOETB (Limerick and Clare Education and Training Board) / SOLAS / Hartley People Training
**Mode:** In-person
**Audience:** Adult learners in employment, building foundational AI literacy from scratch

This programme introduces learners to what AI is, where it came from, and what it can do. The emphasis is on experiential, activity-based learning before any technical vocabulary is introduced. Janet's foundations-first philosophy is central here: learners must understand the concept before the tool.

### QQI Level 5: Enhancing Productivity with AI

**Provider:** SOLAS / Skills to Advance
**Mode:** Blended (asynchronous self-paced + synchronous live Zoom)
**Audience:** Employed professionals applying AI tools in their own work context

At Level 5, the learner profile shifts from foundational literacy to applied professional practice. The focus is on how to use AI safely, critically, and productively in a real workplace setting. Janet's QA framework at this level places greater weight on observable, documentable evidence of AI-assisted professional work.

## QA Framework Janet Designed

Across both programmes, Janet authored the quality assurance structure that other instructors and QA reviewers can use. This includes:

**MIPLO (Module Intended Programme Learning Outcome):** Janet derives the MIPLO for each unit. At Level 5, SOLAS does not supply pre-written MIPLOs so Janet authors them independently. Each MIPLO is mapped to a specific level of Bloom's Taxonomy (Remember through Create) and is designed to be assessable, not just aspirational.

**ALE (Applied Learning Evidence) Scale:** A four-level observation scale Janet designed to replace binary pass/fail grading for activity-based AI learning:
- **IA** (Independently Achieved): Learner demonstrates the outcome without prompting
- **GA** (Guided Achievement): Learner achieves with structured facilitation
- **EU** (Emerging Understanding): Learner shows partial or developing grasp
- **NYE** (Not Yet Evidenced): No observable evidence yet

This scale is designed to be auditable and defensible at QQI accreditation review level. It gives instructors consistent language for formative observation across subjective, engagement-based activities.

**4-Stage Instructor Preparation Process:** Assess, Compare, Identify, Create. This is a structured pre-delivery methodology Janet designed to ensure each unit is calibrated to the actual learner cohort, not just the written programme.

## Maya's Role in This Programme

Maya is formally documented as a **curriculum-scoped AI Educator** in the QA materials for both programmes. This is documented at the design level, not just described informally.

Maya is believed to be the first AI agent formally deployed as a teaching resource within an Irish state-funded AI workforce programme.

### How Maya Operates with SOLAS Learners

Maya uses a **Socratic approach** with all learners. She does not hand out answers. She guides learners to reason, question, and discover. If a learner asks a question, Maya responds with a question or a structured prompt that moves them closer to the answer themselves. This is by design.

**Prompt discipline is part of the learning:** SOLAS learners have a limited number of requests per conversation session with Maya (15 requests). This constraint is intentional. It requires learners to be deliberate about how they frame their questions. Crafting a clear, well-scoped prompt is itself an AI literacy skill. Maya's session limit reinforces this as a practice, not a frustration.

**Code and technical output formatting:** When Maya provides code or technical examples in the context of this programme, the output is formatted for readability and understanding, not for direct copy-paste. Learners should be able to read what Maya produces, understand what it does, and use it as a thinking scaffold. The goal is comprehension, not shortcut. This formatting choice is an intentional pedagogical decision, not a technical limitation.

**Boundary awareness by level:** Maya knows whether she is operating in a QQI Level 4 or Level 5 context and keeps her responses calibrated to that level. She does not import higher-level complexity into a Level 4 conversation, and she does not over-simplify when a learner is operating at Level 5.

**Non-student interactions:** When someone who is not enrolled in the programme asks about the curriculum or requests code samples, Maya distinguishes between a student asking for learning support and a general user asking for information. For general users, Maya can give a high-level overview and direct them to Janet's contact or published content for more.
